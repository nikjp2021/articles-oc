# The Truth About Remote Work in 2026 📊

**Slide 1: The Headline vs. The Reality**
"Remote work is dying" — every headline, 2025-2026.
The reality: 22% of US workers still work remotely — stable since 2022.
That's 3x the pre-pandemic rate. Not a death. A new normal.
📊 Source: Stanford WFH Research / BLS

**Slide 2: The Real Breakdown**
67% of companies offer hybrid or remote flexibility
18% are fully remote
Only 27% are fully in-office
The RTO headlines you see? They're exceptions, not the rule.
📊 Source: Flex Index / Stanford WFH Research

**Slide 3: What Happened When Companies Mandated RTO**
80% admitted to losing talent (ResumeBuilder, 2024)
18% higher turnover among high-performers (Gartner, 2025)
$42K average replacement cost per worker (McKinsey)
The math doesn't add up for mandates.
📊 Source: ResumeBuilder / Gartner / McKinsey

**Slide 4: The Productivity Data They Don't Want You to See**
Randomized control trial in Nature (2024):
→ Hybrid = same productivity as office
→ 33% lower attrition
→ Managers rated both groups equally
Fortune 100 Best Companies (97% remote-friendly): 42% higher productivity
📊 Source: Bloom RCT / Nature / Great Place to Work

**Slide 5: 72 Minutes a Day**
That's what the average remote worker saves on commuting (NBER, 2023).
6 hours a week. 24 hours a month. About 2 full weeks a year.
About 40% goes back into work. The rest? Life.
📊 Source: NBER Working Paper

**Slide 6: The Real Question**
Not "remote vs. office."
It's: Are you measuring output or presence?
The companies winning the talent war offer genuine flexibility and trust their people.
The ones chasing 2019 are losing their best talent to the ones who moved forward.
📊 Source: Gallup / Multiple studies

**Slide 7: The Bottom Line**
Remote work isn't dying.
It's evolving.
And the data says: flexibility works when management does.
