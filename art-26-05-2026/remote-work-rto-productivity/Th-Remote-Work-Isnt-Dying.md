# "Remote Work Is Dying" Is the Best PR Campaign of 2025-2026 🎯

Hot take incoming:

"Remote work is dying" is a narrative manufactured by a handful of legacy CEOs and amplified by a media that loves a good death-of-something story.

The data tells a completely different story. Let me drop some numbers. 🧵👇

**1 /**
22% of US workers still work remotely. That number has been STABLE since late 2022.

Pre-pandemic it was 7%. We're at 3x pre-pandemic levels and holding. That's not a death. That's a new equilibrium.

Source: Stanford WFH Research / BLS

**2 /**
67% of companies still offer hybrid or remote flexibility. Only 27% are fully in-office.

The Amazon/Goldman/Dell mandates make headlines BECAUSE they're the exception, not the rule. Journalism 101: if it bleeds it leads. If it RTOs, it trends.

Source: Flex Index / Stanford

**3 /**
The strongest causal evidence we have? A randomized control trial published in *Nature* (Bloom, 2024).

Result: Hybrid workers = same productivity as office workers. But 33% lower attrition.

33% fewer people quit. That's not a rounding error.

**4 /**
80% of companies with RTO mandates ADMITTED to losing talent because of it (ResumeBuilder, 2024).

Gartner found high-performers were 18% MORE likely to leave at RTO-mandate companies.

The people you want to keep? They have options. And they're using them.

**5 /**
Meanwhile, the Fortune 100 Best Companies — 97% of which are hybrid/remote-friendly — show 42% higher productivity than average.

Not "despite" being flexible. BECAUSE of the management culture that flexibility demands.

**6 /**
Here's the uncomfortable truth:

The RTO push isn't about productivity. If it were, the data would support it. It doesn't.

It's about control. About visible presence. About "I can't manage what I can't see" — which is a management skill issue, not a location issue.

Only 54% of managers trust remote teams (Gallup). That's a coaching problem. Not a WFH problem.

**7 /**
The real future isn't "all remote" or "all office."

It's companies that measure output instead of hours.
It's leaders who trust their teams.
It's work designed around outcomes, not butts in seats.

The companies that figure this out are going to vacuum up all the talent from the ones that don't.
