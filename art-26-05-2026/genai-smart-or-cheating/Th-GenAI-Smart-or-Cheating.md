# 👀 Hot Take: Using AI at work isn't cheating. But lying about it absolutely is.

Let me get personal for a second 🤝

I use AI every single day for my work. First drafts, brainstorming, editing, research — it's in my workflow at almost every stage.

But here's what I don't do:
- I don't pass off AI output as 100% my own without disclosure
- I don't claim skills I can't actually perform without AI
- I don't submit work I haven't read, edited, and verified
- And I DEFINITELY don't lie when someone asks if AI was involved

---

Here's the thing nobody talks about: 🗣️

The calculator analogy is perfect but incomplete.

Using a calculator isn't cheating in the real world. It would be ridiculous to expect an accountant to do long division by hand.

BUT — if that same accountant couldn't tell you when to divide vs. when to multiply? If they couldn't look at the number and say "that doesn't look right"?

That's not an accountant. That's someone pressing buttons.

---

## 🔥 The actual cheating is the skills gap

A Novoresume survey of 1,000 full-time U.S. workers (March 2026) found that 27.2% of AI-using workers have skills listed on their resume they can ONLY perform with significant AI assistance.

Let that sink in.

More than 1 in 4 people are claiming expertise they literally cannot demonstrate without a tool doing the heavy lifting.

And 1 in 7 landed their current job by having AI whisper answers during their interview.

This isn't "working smarter." This is fraud. 💯

---

## 🤔 The line is actually pretty clear

Here's my personal litmus test (I use this every single time):

If someone asked me "Walk me through how you arrived at this conclusion and why you made these choices," could I do it?

If yes → Smart tool usage ✅
If no → I'm outsourcing my judgment ❌

That's it. That's the whole test.

AI can generate the words. AI can suggest the approach. AI can catch my typos.

But AI cannot have my years of experience. AI cannot know my team's specific context. AI cannot take accountability when things go wrong.

And honestly? If you're outsourcing your judgment to AI… you weren't that valuable to begin with. 😬

---

## 🎯 Closing thought

The question isn't "Did you use AI?"

The question is:
"Did you bring anything irreplaceable to this work?"

If the answer is yes, you have nothing to hide.

If the answer is no… you might want to ask yourself why you're getting paid.
