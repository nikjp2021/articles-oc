# 🔥 The Exam Myth: I Scored 92% in Physics. I Couldn't Fix a Lamp.

1/ I topped my high school physics class. 92%. Gold stars. Teacher's pet.

But when the ceiling fan stopped working at home, I stood there like a statue. I had no idea how to diagnose the problem.

That's the moment I realised something was deeply wrong. 🧵

2/ The Dunning-Kruger effect says people with low competence overestimate their ability because they lack the metacognition to know what competence looks like (Kruger & Dunning, 1999).

I was living proof. I thought I understood physics. I understood *exam physics*. Two different things.

3/ Exams reward pattern recognition. You learn to spot the question type, recall the formula, plug in the numbers. Fast. Efficient. Completely divorced from actual understanding.

Bloom's Taxonomy places this at the lowest level: **Remember**. Real STEM work lives at **Evaluate** and **Create**.

4/ Harvard researchers led by Deslauriers found that students in active learning classrooms learned MORE but FELT like they learned less (2019, PNAS).

Why? Because real learning is uncomfortable. It involves struggle, confusion, failure. Exams train you to avoid that feeling. Research-based learning marinates in it.

5/ A 2014 meta-analysis by Freeman et al. looked at 225 STEM studies. Their finding?

Students in active learning were 1.5x LESS likely to fail than those in traditional lecture/exam formats.

The system that feels safest is actually the riskiest.

6/ Self-Determination Theory (Ryan & Deci, 2000) says humans need autonomy, competence, and relatedness to stay motivated.

Exams give you none of those.

Research-based learning gives you all three. You choose your question. You build real skill. You work with others.

7/ A 2026 study by Funa showed that integrated STEM + problem-based learning significantly improved conceptual understanding (SSHO, 13, 102599).

Not recall. *Understanding*. The thing exams claim to measure but rarely do.

8/ So what do we do?

If you're a student — do one research project per semester. Doesn't have to be published. Just real.

If you're an educator — kill the high-stakes final. Use portfolios, lab notebooks, and peer review.

If you're an employer — stop caring about GPAs. Ask candidates how they think.

9/ I'm not saying exams are useless. They measure something — speed, recall, stress tolerance.

But they don't measure what matters most: your ability to face the unknown, ask good questions, and persist through failure.

That's what research teaches. That's what sticks.
