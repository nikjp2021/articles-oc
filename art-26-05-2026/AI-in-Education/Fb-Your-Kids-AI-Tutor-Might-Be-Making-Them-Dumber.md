# 🤔 Your Kid's AI Tutor Might Be Making Them Dumber — Here's What Teachers Are Actually Seeing

👀 **I need to talk to parents about something that's been keeping me up at night.**

My niece is in 7th grade. Last week she showed me how she "writes" her essays now — she types a prompt into ChatGPT, gets a paragraph, rewrites two sentences, and submits. She told me this like it was totally normal. Like this is just what school is now.

And honestly? She's not wrong. A RAND survey from March 2026 found that **62% of U.S. students** — middle school through college — used AI for homework. That's up from 48% just seven months earlier.

![A teenage girl sits at a kitchen table looking uncertain at her laptop screen with a chatbot window open, warm natural light from a window beside her](teen-ai-study.jpg)

But here's the part that really got me. When researchers asked those same students what they thought about AI:

**67% said: "The more students use AI for schoolwork, the more it will harm their critical thinking skills."**

The kids *know*. They feel it happening. They just don't know what to do about it.

📚 **What teachers are seeing in real classrooms:**

The UK's National Education Union surveyed 9,000 teachers in April 2026. Here's what they reported:

🔹 **66% of secondary teachers** say students' critical thinking has visibly worsened with AI use
🔹 **76% of teachers** now use AI tools daily themselves — even as they worry about the effects

One teacher said: *"Students are losing core skills — thinking, creativity, writing, even how to have a conversation."*

Another: *"Children no longer feel the need to spell as voice-to-text replaces knowledge."*

This isn't hypothetical. This is happening in classrooms right now.

🔬 **The science behind why it happens:**

There's a fascinating (and terrifying) study from arXiv this year — a randomized controlled trial with 1,222 people. The finding: after just **10–15 minutes** of using AI assistance, people performed worse when the AI was taken away. They also gave up faster on hard problems.

Think about that. A teenager using AI for 15 minutes is measurably less capable of independent thinking right after.

It's like a cognitive muscle that atrophies when you stop using it.

📊 **But it gets more complicated:**

Here's what I really want parents to understand — because this is the part that isn't making headlines:

✅ AI *can* actually help learning. The big meta-analysis in *Nature* (March 2026) found that GenAI generally improves academic outcomes when used properly.
✅ Estonia just signed a national deal with OpenAI to put AI in all their upper secondary schools — because they believe it can *accelerate* cognitive growth.
✅ Adaptive learning platforms improve student outcomes by about 23% on average (McKinsey/RAND).

The problem isn't AI. The problem is *how* kids are using it.

💡 **What you can do as a parent:**

➡️ **Ask your child's school about their AI policy.** Only 6% of teachers say their school's policies are clear. If your school doesn't have one, ask why.

➡️ **Have the conversation.** Ask your kid: "When do you use AI for school? When do you NOT use it? Do you feel like you're learning or just getting it done faster?"

➡️ **Set "no-AI" zones.** Some assignments should be done without any AI help — just like you'd expect your kid to learn multiplication before using a calculator.

![A caring mother sitting beside her middle-school child at a desk, gently pointing at a notebook while a smartphone sits face-down, symbolising no-AI zones](parental-guidance.jpg)

➡️ **Watch for the signs.** If your kid can't start an assignment without opening ChatGPT first, that's a red flag.
