# 🔥 We spent 3 years panicking about AI cheating. The real problem is worse: students are just... stopping thinking.

![Close-up of a student's hand holding a pen over a blank notebook, frozen mid-air, soft screen glow from an unseen laptop casting blue light across the page](stalled-thinking.jpg)

**Caption:** The data is clear. AI boosts performance WITH the tool, but erodes it WITHOUT. 67% of students agree it harms their critical thinking. Yet 86% of schools keep deploying it. We're watching a generation lose the ability to think independently. 🧵👇

---

1/
🚨 We spent 2023–2024 panicking about plagiarism.
Detectors, honor codes, "AI is cheating" declarations.

Turns out that was the wrong worry.

2/
The real concern isn't that students use AI to cheat.
It's that they use AI *even when trying to learn* —
and it's making their brains weaker. 🧠

3/
📊 Here's the data:
ALEKS platform studied 3.2M student interactions.
Findings:
→ Learning time dropped 31% among high schoolers
→ Retention scores declined 25%
→ Students learned *less* even when they thought they were learning more

https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21629

4/
⏱️ RCT with 1,222 people:
After just 10–15 MINUTES of AI use, people performed worse WITHOUT the AI and gave up faster on hard problems.

10–15 minutes.

https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04721

5/
👩‍🏫 Teachers are watching it happen in real time.
66% of UK secondary teachers say critical thinking has worsened (NEU Survey, April 2026).

Quote from one teacher:
*"Students are losing core skills — thinking, creativity, writing, even how to have a conversation."*

6/
😳 But here's the most alarming stat:
67% of STUDENTS themselves believe AI harms their critical thinking (RAND, March 2026).

The people using the tools are telling us it's hurting them.
And we're not listening.

7/
🏛️ Meanwhile, the governance gap:
→ 86% of education orgs keep deploying AI (Microsoft 2025)
→ Only 6% of teachers say policies are clear (Stanford HAI 2026)
→ 76% of teachers use AI daily but worry about it (NEU 2026)

Total governance gap.

8/
🌍 Estonia gets it right:
They signed a national OpenAI deal NOT to replace thinking, but to *augment* it.
Education Minister: *"The challenge is how to put AI into the learning process so it accelerates cognitive growth."*

That's the ONLY approach that works.

9/
Hot take:
AI in education isn't good or bad.
It's a cognitive lever. 

Use it to REPLACE thinking → students get dumber.
Use it to AUGMENT thinking → students get smarter.

Most schools are doing the first and calling it the second.

10/
🛠️ We don't need more AI tools.
We need frameworks for INTENTIONAL use.
Wellesley's 8-level spectrum. Chandler's 5-tier system.
Policies where teachers decide per-assignment (Columbus).

That's the path forward.

End of thread. What's your school doing about AI? 🎯
