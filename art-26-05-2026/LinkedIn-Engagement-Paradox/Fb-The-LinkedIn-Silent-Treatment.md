# 😶 The LinkedIn Silent Treatment — Why Your Connections Won't Like Your Posts (But Will Engage a Stranger's)

![Warm lifestyle photograph of a woman in a bright coffee shop looking thoughtfully at her phone showing a LinkedIn post with low engagement.](fb-silent-treatment.jpg)

You poured two hours into that post. You hit publish. You wait.

Your mom likes it. Your old colleague from three jobs ago leaves a 🔥. And… silence.

Meanwhile, you scroll past a near-identical take from someone you've never met — and you hit like without thinking. So does everyone else.

**Sound familiar?** You're not imagining it. Here's why.

---

**It starts with the algorithm.** LinkedIn's 2026 data shows reach dropped ~50% year-over-year (van der Blom, 2026). Only **31% of the average feed** comes from people you know — LinkedIn shifted from a Relationship Graph to an Interest Graph (Goodman, 2026). Your connections might not even *see* your post.

But even when they do, psychology kicks in.

**1. The comparison trap.** ![Warm digital illustration of a person at a desk with a thought bubble splitting into two scenes: one showing a celebration and the other showing peaceful learning.](fb-comparison-trap.jpg) A 2026 study found professionals who see peer achievements on LinkedIn daily report **34% lower job satisfaction** (Sheikh, 2026). Engaging with your post means publicly acknowledging your success while measuring their own career against it. With a stranger? No comparison baseline.

**2. The imposter effect.** Researchers at the University of Edinburgh proved LinkedIn usage triggers imposter thoughts (Marder et al., 2025). Your connection's brain whispers: *"If I engage, will they compare our careers?"* Fear of Negative Evaluation is the #1 predictor of social avoidance online (Tuerxun & Zou, 2026). With a stranger, there's no shared history — engaging feels safe.

**3. The friend vs. marketer conflict.** Studies show we evaluate people as *both* friends and marketers simultaneously (Chen et al., 2026). A like to you is loaded: *"I endorse your standing AND accept the unspoken expectation."* A like to a stranger is simple: *"I found this useful."*

---

**What to actually do about it:**

**Don't take it personally.** This is neurobiology, not a referendum on your content.

**Lower the barrier.** ![Close-up lifestyle shot of hands writing A/B/C options in a notebook with a phone next to it showing a LinkedIn poll post.](fb-low-barrier.jpg) A/B/C polls beat "What do you think?" — low-friction questions bypass the fear response.

**Build topic authority.** LinkedIn's 360Brew AI rewards consistency on 2-3 themes. Focus = reach.

**Write for the stranger.** Your first two sentences should make your topic unmistakable. That's what feeds the Interest Graph.
